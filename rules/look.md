# Look and graphics

- Visuals must be YouTube-native, not web design: Claude over-uses labels and text; keep text minimal.
- Light theme as the main look; replace the old dark/teal background behind the screen + A-roll layout. In that layout the A-roll card goes on the RIGHT (he looks to his right at the screen).
- Fonts: EverygenNeue (brand font) plus Hedvig Letters Serif. Use Hedvig SPARINGLY, only where a graphic should feel Everygen-branded; basic text is not Hedvig.
- Progression graphic: LOCKED (2026-11-08). Workflow-graph modules with the Gemini logo, alternating top/bottom rows, frosted-glass hidden steps (never readable; a reveal only goes frosted → clear). Its background is unique to it; other full-screen visuals share a different look that stays the same from video to video.
- Motion: everything enters from the bottom, with the house `materialize` entrance. Colour, font and easing come from the Everygen design system; YouTube visuals stay simpler than the website.
- Overlays on A-roll (keep as is): placed beside his face, never over it, with a big black feathered shape on the left as a vignette behind them. Numbered lists with glossy blue badges + Hedvig, Inter question text with a key word in blue Hedvig, white chat-input card with highlighted words, app icons popping in.
- Full-screen explainers (e.g. "shot map"): simple, digestible concept visuals; he wants more inventive ones from code.
- Feel: magical, UI-styled, "AI software company". Keep compositions centered so eyes don't jump.
- Everything that matters stays about 6% in from the frame edges (2026-11-11: UI pieces and thumbnails touching the edges looked wrong).
- Motion level (2026-11-11): about 6/10, a polished YouTube explainer, not a motion-design reel. Springy entrances with a slight overshoot and better-designed layouts did the most; subtle camera drift is fine; heavy 3D tilts look cheap; no motion blur (too slow to render). Hard cuts stay the default, with simple designed hand-offs (A-roll falls back, or shrinks into a card) at big moments.
- Centered at every moment, not just in the final layout: when an element enters later (a timeline under two clips, a second card beside the first), whatever is on screen before it is centered and sized for the space it has alone (larger, vertically centered), then re-lays out (house spring, ω 7.5) to make room as the new element enters. Never leave empty space waiting for something that hasn't arrived yet. The same goes for a key image inside a screen recording: center it (open it big) rather than leaving it at the edge of the window.
- Rebuild screen recordings in code where it looks cleaner; recreate Everygen's generation animation faithfully.
- Proof: a concrete reference (a tool, an output, a stat, a past video) shows the real thing: the real UI or output, which may be rebuilt in code as long as it stays that same thing. Invented concept visuals are only for abstract ideas.
- Proof, from the video-13 tests (2026-11-11):
  - Social proof (how many people use something, a trend, a reaction) is always the real thing from social media, such as real YouTube thumbnails or X posts, never a designed visualization of a crowd.
  - A real thing is shown the way the viewer meets it in real life: a website in a clean browser window, a post as the post, a video in its player.
  - Zoom to the part that backs the line, whole and centered, big enough to read in the time it's on screen; isolate the elements that matter from a big block of text or data. (Fable's zoom on the announcement page cut off its top and left the headline off-center; a whole benchmark table flashed by.)
  - When the line restates what a source says, highlight the source's own sentence. Never change its words: "the most powerful Claude model to date" is backed by the page's real "our most capable models".
- The speaker is on screen in the first frame of the video (full-screen or small, as long as he's visible).
- Screen + A-roll layout: `presets/layouts/screen-aroll` (the whole app window with its own corners, menu bar cropped off, A-roll card on the right). Never a frame that cuts into the recording.
- His camera as an inset is never a 16:9 corner window (it looks lazy): use the vertical A-roll card from the screen + A-roll preset, vertically centred on the right, with clear space between it and the content.
- A clip played full screen (an ad, a result) gets a progress bar: Everygen blue, full width, flush to the bottom, about 25 px at 1080, eased slightly into its end so it reads faster at the start (a retention hack).
- Every overlay carries Everygen blue somewhere (a blue pill, a blue key word): plain white text alone doesn't read as Everygen.
- Screen captures: light mode, a clean UI (no clutter, incognito-style), 150-175% browser zoom so punch-ins stay crisp, and the same capture frame across a set so they swap cleanly.
- Pricing on screen: the numbers he says in that video's script, laid out like the pricing screenshot he gives for it (`brand/library/`). They A/B test the site, so never copy the live page or an older video (2026-11-12: the old "always Basic $14" rule is gone; video 13 uses annual Starter $9, 200 credits).
- Comparing two versions (two models' edits, before/after): always the same moment in both, each on a fixed side for the whole video, and always clear which one is on screen (video 13: Opus left, Fable right).
- Camera beside a played video (video 13, his pick of three options): one big player, a segmented switch above it naming whose it is, the timecode in a scrub bar UNDER the video (a timecode pill on the picture read as part of the video), his camera as the vertical card on the right.
- "Value graphic" (his term, 2026-11-12): one screen that explains a whole pricing or offer section, its elements arriving one by one as he names them, like the progression graphic. It may leave and come back.
- Framing a screen recording (his pass-2 notes on video 13, his strongest complaint): at every moment the thing the viewer should look at (what the mouse is doing, the window that just opened, the box being typed in) is whole, readable and centred in the free area; never in a corner, cut by the frame or under his card. A newly opened window or page is shown whole at normal scale first. A strong zoom is fine when its target stays whole and centred (he praised 1.7x on a Finder window); a target in a corner of the window gets a magnifying glass, not a zoom. Zoom out to normal before moving to the next target. Details in `presets/layouts/screen-aroll/README.md`.
- Normal scale of a screen recording (his final notes on video 13, "remember this as a normal scale for now and for future videos as well", said about 1:37): the whole app window at the layout's rest size (96.2% of the frame width, centred) with his card on the right, exactly as in `presets/layouts/screen-aroll/README.md`. Whenever nothing needs pointing at (a message just sent, a chat working, a new chat opened), the recording sits exactly there. Don't slide or shrink the window to clear the card: he corrected two such moments back to normal.
- A magnifying glass stays up for the whole phrase it belongs to (he asked for longer on the footage length). It sits on the exact spot the line names, found on the full-resolution image (video 14 test: "pure white spots" are on her chin and around her mouth; Claude put it on her forehead).
- Every image at its best quality: the original file (downloaded post, the Everygen library), never a thumbnail or a screen-recording crop blown up; a video example plays as a video (video 14 test, 2:22).
- Never an empty frame between visuals: the next one arrives while the last one leaves (video 14 test: 1.6 s of blank background at 1:19).
- A screen recording is cropped on the window's own edges, its top bar whole, its corners matching the app's own (no extra rounding), and no line of text cut at the frame edge (video 14 run 2: 1:55 cut through the browser's top bar with big invented corners; 6:05 cut a chat line at the top).
- A long run of full-screen visuals (about 15 s or more) keeps him in a card beside them (video 14 test: no A-roll from 1:20 to 1:50).
- Overlays beside him: the shot is reframed so he and the graphics balance as one picture, and nothing covers him (video 14 test, 0:36: two cards squeezed beside a centred speaker, the "?" card over his arm). This is design sense, not a fixed scale.
- A rewind or flashback through a recording is black and white and slightly washed out while it rewinds, and returns to normal colour as the present resumes (video 13: when he is about to send the prompt and the "rewinding" pill fades).
- A zoom on a finished graphic keeps the whole graphic and its white space in view; to point at a detail use a magnifying glass on it (video 13: Opus's wrong logo).
- Creative subtitles sit at the BOTTOM of the screen, one centred line, white EverygenNeue with a soft shadow, the key words on an Everygen-blue pill (his pick of three, video 13). Not stacked beside him.
- A graphic that carries his camera in a card (the progression graphic) plays the live A-roll of that moment in the card and hands over to and from full-screen A-roll by growing or shrinking that card.
- Comment cards in a Claude doc: Claude's default avatar (blue circle, white initials "AL"), "Artem Linov", a grey time.
- Don't flag the staging login in the editing doc; he knows.
