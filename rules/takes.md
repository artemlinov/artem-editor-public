# Take selection

Artem records one long A-roll with retakes, reading the script off his screen.

**Claude never changes the script.** No cutting, tightening, reordering or rewording of scripted lines. The only selection decision Claude makes is which take to use, by the rules below, with no exceptions.

- Default: use the LAST take of each sentence. Takes can be mixed at sentence boundaries, and what he actually said beats the script wording (confirmed 2026-11-07).
- A take only counts if it starts at the sentence's real beginning. To get going he often runs up from the end of the previous sentence (e.g. says "...an actual girl in her living room isn't what you'd think. And it's worth knowing..." when targeting the second sentence). That run-up is NOT a take of the previous sentence: use the previous sentence's earlier full take, and start the clip at "And it's worth knowing" (if that is its last take).
- Warm-ups: he may say the whole sentence twice (use the last), or only a fragment of it ("for a single video like this") before the real take: start at the real take.
- Stumbles inside the last take: cut the stumble out, keep the take (rare; he usually re-records).
- If a later partial redo of a sentence might be a warm-up rather than a real take, don't guess: ask him with timecodes, then turn the answer into a rule.
- A later slow pickup that breaks the line into pieces ("that's exactly what drives the voice" … pause … "Consistency.") is not a take, even if it's the last one and closer to the script wording. Use his last full, fluent take of the sentence (video 12, 07:51: "That's the exact thing that drives the voice consistency.").
- Track gaze per take, measured only during the real take, not the warm-up (he always looks at the script monitor while warming up): looking at camera vs at screen. Screen looks are staged to seem like he's working on screen (he isn't, screen recs are made afterwards); they suit the screen + A-roll layout, and some doc notes depend on them (e.g. a take where he looks at the screen after a line).
- Hidden retakes: he often reads a line off the script monitor, then re-delivers it to camera straight away with almost no pause (0.15-0.45 s). Whisper drops an immediate repeat, so the pair transcribes as ONE sentence and the real last take is invisible. Before accepting a take: transcribe the speech that runs on after its out-point (up to the next 0.6 s pause); if it repeats the sentence, that repeat is the last take. And a chosen take where he looks at the screen on a line meant for camera is almost always that read-through: look for the camera take right after it.

- A slip of the tongue disqualifies a take, even the last one: use the last CLEAN take (video 13: my pick said "Opens gets just as far"; he swapped in the take before it). Read each chosen take's transcript for a wrong or mangled word before accepting it.
- Two lines he says in one breath stay together: when a line runs straight on into the next one in some take, use that take for both, not the last take of each (video 13: "And normally… revisions. With Everygen, you skip all of that…"; he asked for "the take where I said both of these phrases in one take").

**Why:** his recording habit, explained 2026-11-07. Hidden retakes: video 12, 2026-11-09, 13 of 90 picks were the screen read-through (he caught two, at 2:13 and 2:36).
**How to apply:** every cut of his A-roll.
